First concept

The same pulse rate can mean different things

A pulse of 120 after running is expected. A pulse of 120 at rest with chest pain and faintness is a different situation. Context changes meaning.

120

Fast but expected

Exercise, fever, pain, fear, or excitement can raise the pulse as part of a normal body response.

!

Fast and concerning

A sudden or persistent fast pulse at rest with chest discomfort, shortness of breath, dizziness, syncope, weakness, or poor perfusion needs evaluation.

What may cause the finding?

Common pulse problems

↑

Tachycardia

May occur with exercise, fever, pain, anxiety, dehydration, blood loss, anemia, infection, stimulants, medications, or an arrhythmia.

↓

Bradycardia

May be normal during sleep or in trained athletes, or may occur with medications, conduction disease, electrolyte problems, thyroid disease, or heart damage.

≈

Irregular pulse

May feel like skipped beats, fluttering, or uneven spacing. Premature beats and atrial fibrillation are examples of rhythm problems.

~

Weak or thready

Can accompany low stroke volume, dehydration, bleeding, shock, or poor peripheral perfusion.

Treatment basics

Treatment follows the cause and the patient’s stability

Sinus tachycardia

Clinicians generally look for and treat the cause—such as fever, pain, dehydration, blood loss, anxiety, hypoxia, or infection—rather than simply slowing the heart rate.

Symptomatic bradycardia

Evaluation may include reviewing medications and correcting reversible causes. Severe or prolonged cases may require specialized treatment or a pacemaker.

Arrhythmia

Treatment depends on the rhythm and symptoms and may involve monitoring, medication, cardioversion, ablation, or an implanted device under medical care.

EMS learner note: Treatment decisions depend on the rhythm, perfusion, symptoms, local protocol, and medical direction. A pulse alone does not identify the rhythm.

Scenario practice

Choose the best interpretation

Scenario 1

A healthy runner has a resting pulse of 52, regular and strong, with no symptoms.

Scenario 2

A patient has a pulse of 132, weak and regular, with dry mucous membranes and dizziness after vomiting.

Scenario 3

A patient suddenly feels a rapid irregular pulse with chest pressure, shortness of breath, and near-syncope.

Red flags

When the pulse finding becomes more concerning

Chest pain or pressureMay indicate a cardiac emergency, especially with a sudden fast, slow, or irregular pulse.
Shortness of breathCan mean the rhythm is affecting cardiac output or may accompany another serious illness.
Dizziness, fainting, or confusionMay signal reduced blood flow to the brain and poor perfusion.
Weakness, sweating, pallor, or collapseLook for shock, severe arrhythmia, bleeding, infection, or another emergency cause.

Treat the patient, not the BPM

Rate, rhythm, strength, equality, symptoms, perfusion, and the cause all work together. An ECG is needed to identify many rhythm problems.
