Free pre-course foundation

Get ready for EMT school before day one

Build the language, anatomy, vital-sign, assessment, communication, and study foundations that make a fast-paced EMT course easier to enter and easier to manage.

No account requiredProgress saves on your deviceFour- or six-week planNot a replacement for EMT education

This prepares you for EMT school—it does not replace it

EMSCodeSim’s EMT Prep Program is a free readiness resource for future students. It does not provide certification, course credit, protocol authorization, clinical training, or a substitute for a state-approved EMT education program. Your EMT instructors should remain the source of truth for course requirements, skills, treatment standards, and local protocols.

What this program covers

You will build

  • Basic medical vocabulary and directional language
  • A practical overview of major body systems
  • Understanding of what common vital signs represent
  • Familiarity with the patient-assessment sequence
  • Study, communication, and time-management habits
  • A realistic picture of EMT-school expectations

You will not be asked to

  • Memorize a complete EMT textbook before class
  • Learn local treatment protocols on your own
  • Practice invasive or medication skills without instruction
  • Replace hands-on lab time with an online page
  • Use this program as proof of certification or competency

0 of 6 modules

Choose your pace

Spend about 20–35 minutes per session. The goal is familiarity and confidence, not mastery before class begins.

1

Understand what EMT school will ask of you

Start with realistic expectations so the workload is not a surprise.

Knowledge

Expect reading, lectures, vocabulary, quizzes, written exams, and frequent connection between body systems and patient findings.

Hands-on skills

Skills improve through repeated, coached practice. Watching a video or reading a checklist is not the same as performing under instructor observation.

Patient assessment

EMT school asks you to organize safety, immediate threats, history, examination, vital signs, treatment, communication, and reassessment.

Time

Plan for class, travel, reading, assignments, skills lab, clinical or field time, and several short review sessions each week.

Do this before moving on

  • Put all known class dates and obligations on one calendar.
  • Choose two or three repeatable weekly study blocks.
  • Identify transportation, childcare, work, or technology problems early.
  • Make a plan to ask for help quickly instead of waiting until the end of the course.

3

Build anatomy and physiology foundations

Anatomy is structure. Physiology is function. EMT school will connect both to what you see, hear, feel, and measure.

Respiratory

Moves air and supports oxygen and carbon-dioxide exchange.

Know: airway, lungs, diaphragm, ventilation, oxygenation.

Cardiovascular

Circulates blood to transport oxygen, nutrients, heat, and waste.

Know: heart, arteries, veins, capillaries, perfusion.

Nervous

Controls consciousness, sensation, movement, and many automatic body functions.

Know: brain, spinal cord, nerves, mental status.

Musculoskeletal

Provides structure, movement, protection, and support.

Know: bones, joints, muscles, tendons, ligaments.

Integumentary

The skin protects the body and helps regulate temperature and fluid loss.

Know: color, temperature, moisture, wounds, burns.

Digestive

Breaks down food and absorbs nutrients and fluids.

Know: mouth, esophagus, stomach, intestines, liver.

Endocrine

Uses hormones to regulate metabolism, glucose, stress responses, and other functions.

Know: pancreas, insulin, glucose, adrenal response.

Urinary

Filters blood, removes waste, and helps control fluid and electrolyte balance.

Know: kidneys, ureters, bladder, urine output.

A useful pre-course question

For each body system, ask: What is its main job, and what might I observe if that job is not working well? Do not try to diagnose every disease before your course teaches you how to assess patients safely.

6

Build student habits before the course begins

The best preparation is a dependable learning system—not trying to memorize the entire EMT curriculum early.

Read actively

Preview headings and objectives, then close the material and explain the main idea from memory.

Practice retrieval

Use short quizzes, flashcards, drawing, and teaching aloud instead of rereading the same paragraph repeatedly.

Use an error log

Write what you missed, why you missed it, and what clue should lead you to the better answer next time.

Communicate professionally

Practice introductions, respectful questions, listening, closed-loop communication, and accepting correction.

Protect your schedule

Frequent 20-minute sessions are usually more useful than waiting for one exhausted all-night study session.

Ask for help early

Use instructors, tutoring, skills lab, classmates, and approved resources before a small gap becomes a major problem.
