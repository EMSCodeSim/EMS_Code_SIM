A practical four-week review plan

  1. Week 1 — Assessment foundations

    Review scene size-up, primary assessment, SAMPLE/OPQRST, and vital signs. Use the ABC center and full vitals practice daily.

  2. Week 2 — Medical & airway

    Rotate asthma, stroke, hypoglycemia scenarios and breath-sound recognition. Take the daily EMT quiz and study missed topics.

  3. Week 3 — Trauma & operations

    Practice trauma scenarios, splinting logic, hemorrhage/shock concepts, consent/refusal, and documentation with the PCR narrative coach.

  4. Week 4 — Mixed review & psychomotor alignment

    Take a timed practice exam, use today’s 5-minute practice, repeat weak flashcards, and walk through official skill sheets before your practical exam.

What to expect on cognitive test day

The EMT cognitive exam is a computer-based assessment covering airway, cardiology, trauma, medical, OB/GYN, and operations. Question counts and time limits follow current National Registry rules—confirm details on the official NREMT site before you schedule. Treat every practice exam as a pacing rehearsal: read carefully, avoid rushing early items, and flag tough questions only if your practice tool allows review.

Computerized adaptive testing (when used) adjusts difficulty based on your responses. You cannot “save hard questions for later” the way you might on a paper test. Focus on fundamentals, eliminate wrong answers, and do not chase obscure trivia at the expense of ABC and scene-safety logic.

Common miss patterns (and how to fix them)

  • Airway vs breathing confusion: Obstruction, snoring, and stridor are airway problems; wheezes and crackles are usually lower-airway/breathing findings. Drill with the ABC center and breath sound center.
  • Compensated shock: Students wait for hypotension. Practice recognizing early perfusion failure using skin, pulse quality, mentation, and mechanism on the trauma center.
  • Medication indications: Know when oxygen, aspirin, epinephrine, naloxone, glucose, and albuterol are indicated—and when they are not—using your textbook and protocol, then reinforce with quizzes.
  • Operations & ethics: Consent, refusal, DNR/POLST awareness, documentation, and scene safety are frequently under-studied. Use the protocol drill and PCR coach.

Align cognitive study with psychomotor skills

Many students separate “book knowledge” from skill sheets. Better approach: after each weak cognitive topic, run the matching skill once. Missed trauma items → hemorrhage control and rapid trauma assessment sheets. Missed medical items → medical assessment sheet plus a scenario. Missed airway items → oxygen and BVM practice with a partner or class lab.

NREMT cognitive prep FAQ

Is EMSCodeSim affiliated with NREMT?

No. This is an independent free study site. Follow your approved program and official National Registry guidance for eligibility, scheduling, and scoring.

How many practice exams should I take?

One timed exam early to find weak domains, then topic drills, then another timed exam in the final week is a practical pattern for most students.

Should I only memorize practice questions?

No. Memorizing items without understanding ABC priorities, shock, and assessment sequences fails when wording changes. Study the concept, then retest.

What free tools should I use this week?

Start with the timed practice exam, then the daily EMT quiz, flashcards, and one simulator from the training tools library.
