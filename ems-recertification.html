NREMT certification and state licensure are separate

National Registry certification demonstrates national competency. Your state license or certification gives you legal authorization to practice. Their expiration dates, fees, education rules, skills verification, and renewal portals may be different.

Two renewal methods

Choose continuing education or recertification by examination

01

Continuing education

Complete the required NCCP credits during your current certification cycle, enter or import the education into your National Registry account, complete the application, obtain required verification, and submit before expiration.

  • Best for clinicians completing education throughout the cycle.
  • Allows national, local/state, and individual education to document continued competency.
  • Education must be completed before the certification expiration date.
02

Recertification by examination

Apply for recertification by examination, receive an Authorization to Test, and pass the certification examination during the permitted testing window.

  • One attempt is allowed between April 1 one year before expiration and March 31 of the expiration year.
  • Passing by examination initially renews the credential in inactive status.
  • Agency and medical-director steps may be needed to return to active status.

EMT and paramedic timeline

Know the important National Registry dates

April 1

One year before expiration

Recertification by examination opens.

October 1

Six months before expiration

Continuing-education renewal applications begin being accepted.

March 25

Examination application deadline

National Registry identifies this as the RBE application deadline to allow scheduling before expiration.

March 31

Certification expiration date

Final day to submit the normal continuing-education application. All CE must already be complete.

April 1–30

Reinstatement period

A late application may be submitted with the additional reinstatement fee only when the education was completed by March 31.

May 1

Certification lapses

The clinician must use the appropriate National Registry pathway to regain certification.

Your state credential may use a different date. Place both dates on your calendar.

Before paying for a class

Confirm that the education will count

Commonly accepted sources

  • Education approved by a U.S. State EMS Office or designated authorizing agency.
  • CAPCE-accredited EMS continuing education.
  • Qualified education from EMS education programs.
  • Eligible accredited college courses or alternative credits under National Registry policy.
  • Online or in-person education; National Registry currently states there is no online-hour limit.

Examples that may not count

  • Duplicate courses within the same certification cycle.
  • Clinical rotations or routine performance of duty.
  • Preceptor hours, skills-examiner service, or agency volunteer time.
  • Instructor courses and management or leadership courses that do not meet accepted clinical education rules.
  • Education that is not directly related to EMS patient care.

Status and affiliation

Understand active and inactive National Registry status

Active status

Active status is for clinicians currently providing patient care at their certification level. EMTs generally need agency affiliation and skills verification. Paramedics need agency affiliation plus verification by the training officer and medical director.

Inactive status

Inactive status can help clinicians who are not currently practicing, are between agencies, are working in education or administration, or cannot obtain skills verification. Continuing-education requirements still apply, and inactive status does not replace state renewal.
