EMS CAREER & CERTIFICATION GUIDE · Updated October 20, 2026

How to renew your NREMT certification

A practical, detailed guide for EMRs, EMTs, AEMTs and paramedics. Compare requirements, plan your continuing education and follow the application from start to approval.

See required hours ↓ · Renewal checklist ↓ · Official references ↓

Important: National Registry certification is not the same as state licensure. Your state may have separate education, deadlines and renewal steps. This independent EMSCodeSim guide is not affiliated with or endorsed by NREMT. Always follow the current requirements shown in your official NREMT account.

1. How many CE hours do I need?

NREMT's National Continued Competency Program (NCCP) divides qualifying education into National (50%), Local/State (25%) and Individual (25%) components. The applicable 2025 NCCP national topic model must also be satisfied.

Paramedic30151560

National component

Complete the specific national topics required for your certification level. Airway, cardiovascular care, trauma, medical emergencies and operations may be represented; the exact distribution varies by level and NCCP version. Total hours alone are not proof that every required topic is satisfied. Check the official NCCP topic breakdown.

Local or state component

Complete approved education that addresses local system needs, state rules, medical director priorities or regional protocols. When no local topics are specified, consult NREMT rules for allowable education.

Individual component

Choose eligible EMS-related education to address personal learning needs. Examples include clinical assessment, pediatric emergencies and advanced airway management, where appropriate for the certification level.

2. Step-by-step renewal process

  1. Sign in: Open My NREMT and check your credential, expiration date, contact information and application eligibility.
  2. Verify your affiliation: Check agency information. Clinicians not currently using EMS skills may need to select inactive status.
  3. Review your transcript: Compare entered and imported continuing education against national topics and component requirements.
  4. Complete missing CE: Take qualifying courses within the applicable certification cycle and retain completion documentation.
  5. Assign hours: Place education into appropriate components and topics; do not double-count the same credit.
  6. Submit the application: Complete required declarations, review details and pay the current NREMT fee.
  7. Obtain verification: Active renewal may require authorized agency or medical director verification, depending on level.
  8. Confirm approval: Monitor pending actions, audit notices and your updated certification expiration date. Submitting is not the same as approval.

Dates refer to your expiration year. A late CE application may incur an additional fee; CE completed after expiration cannot be used for that expired cycle. Never assume a late application extends authorization to practice. Check current dates and reinstatement rules.

4. What education qualifies?

Qualifying CAPCE-accredited or state-accepted EMS education, eligible EMS academic coursework and certain standardized certification courses may count. Online education can qualify, subject to content and acceptance rules. Duty shifts, clinical rotations, preceptor time, duplicate education and unrelated training should not be assumed to count. Teaching eligible EMS content may qualify under specific instructor-credit rules. Keep records for potential audit. Explore CAPCE.

5. Renew by examination instead

Eligible clinicians can choose recertification by examination. Apply during the designated window, pay the fee, schedule and pass the applicable exam. The examination pathway generally provides one attempt. Passing initially results in inactive status until applicable active-status requirements are met. If unsuccessful, CE renewal may remain possible only if its requirements are completed before expiration. Consult the official handbook before selecting this route.

6. Active, inactive and lapsed certification

Active: For clinicians using EMS skills with appropriate affiliation and verification. Inactive: Preserves national certification for eligible clinicians not currently practicing; it does not authorize practice. Returning to active status requires the applicable process. Lapsed: After the renewal and reinstatement windows close, follow NREMT's level-specific process to regain certification; it may involve education, examination and other documentation.

7. Plan your two-year cycle

Start early: target roughly 25% of CE in the first six months, 50% by the first year, 80% by month 18, and finish before the application deadline. Review national topic gaps periodically rather than tracking only total hours. This schedule is a planning suggestion, not an NREMT requirement.

9. Frequently asked questions

Does NREMT renewal renew my state license?

No. Verify your state license requirements independently, including dates, fees and CE topics.

Can I complete all CE online?

Qualifying online education can count. Check each provider and course against current NREMT and state requirements.

Can I count a course twice?

No. Eligible hours may be assigned where allowed, but the same credit cannot be counted twice.

Does CPR count?

Some qualifying CPR and standardized certification education may count within NREMT rules. Verify the accepted hours and topic allocation.

What if my renewal is pending?

Check the My NREMT dashboard for outstanding agency verification, audits, returned applications or payment issues. Submission alone does not mean approved renewal.

What if I miss the deadline?

Review the limited late-application period and level-specific regaining-certification procedures in the official handbook. Do not assume you may continue practicing on an expired credential.
