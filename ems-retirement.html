Prepare for life after the ambulance

Retirement planning for EMS should include money, benefits, health, identity, family, credentials, and a meaningful transition—not just a final work date.

Start years before your final shift

A good retirement plan is built through small decisions: understanding your pension or account, protecting health, reducing debt, maintaining options, and deciding what role EMS should have in your next chapter.

The EMS retirement framework

Benefits

Know your pension formula, vesting, survivor options, retiree health coverage, deferred compensation, and unused leave rules.

Savings

Track employer accounts, IRAs, emergency savings, debt, taxes, and the cost of healthcare before Medicare eligibility.

Health

Address injuries, sleep, hearing, cardiovascular risk, mental health, and disability documentation before separation.

Identity

Build purpose and relationships outside the uniform before work is removed from the daily routine.

Transition work

Teaching, simulation, consulting, part-time clinical work, emergency management, volunteer service, or a different field.

Family plan

Agree on lifestyle, location, caregiving, travel, work, insurance, and spending expectations.

Questions for your benefits office

  • When am I vested, and what service credit is recognized?
  • How is the retirement benefit calculated?
  • What changes if I leave before normal retirement eligibility?
  • Which survivor-benefit choices are available and when must I elect them?
  • How are overtime, specialty pay, or final compensation treated?
  • What health insurance options exist after separation and before Medicare?
  • What happens to unused leave?
  • Can I work for the same or another public employer after retirement?
  • How do disability retirement and workers’ compensation interact with retirement benefits?
  • What documents should I retain permanently?

A timeline for the final ten years

Time remainingPriorities
10+ yearsIncrease savings rate, understand pension rules, reduce high-interest debt, build non-EMS skills, and protect health.
5–10 yearsEstimate retirement income and expenses, review healthcare gap, update beneficiaries, plan housing, and test transition-work ideas.
2–5 yearsRequest formal benefit estimates, organize records, review taxes, evaluate leave use, and build a detailed post-retirement routine.
Final yearConfirm dates and elections in writing, complete required applications, coordinate insurance, and prepare emotionally for the transition.
After retirementReview income, taxes, healthcare, purpose, relationships, and whether part-time work supports or undermines the plan.

Build a retirement document file

  • Employment history and service-credit records.
  • Pension or retirement account statements.
  • Beneficiary elections and survivor-option documents.
  • Collective bargaining agreements or benefit summaries relevant to retirement.
  • Workers’ compensation, exposure, injury, disability, and medical records you are entitled to retain.
  • Licenses, certifications, teaching credentials, résumé, and professional portfolio.
  • Life, disability, long-term care, and health insurance information.
  • Estate documents and emergency contacts.

Retirement readiness checklist

Financial and benefits rules are personal

Public pensions, Social Security coverage, tax treatment, retiree health plans, and re-employment restrictions vary widely. Use qualified benefits, tax, legal, and financial professionals who can review your actual situation.

Career health matters

Retirement readiness includes the health and relationships you will bring into retirement.
