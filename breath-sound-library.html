Sound families

Sort sounds by continuity and location

Vesicular

Soft, low-pitched sounds heard over most peripheral lung tissue. Inspiration is longer and louder than expiration.

Bronchovesicular

Medium pitch and intensity, commonly heard near the upper sternum and between the scapulae.

Bronchial / tracheal

Louder and higher-pitched, expected over the trachea or central airways—not normally over peripheral lung fields.

Wheeze

High-pitched, musical, continuous sound caused by airflow through narrowed airways; often louder on expiration.

Rhonchi

Lower-pitched, coarse, snoring-like continuous sound associated with turbulent airflow or secretions in larger airways. It may change after coughing.

Pleural friction rub

Grating or creaking sound linked to inflamed pleural surfaces rubbing together. It may be heard during both inspiration and expiration.

Fine crackles

Brief, high-pitched, delicate popping sounds, often late inspiratory and sometimes compared with separating fine hook-and-loop fastener.

Coarse crackles

Louder, lower-pitched bubbling or popping sounds, often associated with larger airways, secretions, or fluid.

Key distinction

Crackles are individual interrupted events. Wheezes and rhonchi continue for a longer portion of the breath.

Stridor

Harsh, high-pitched sound from upper-airway narrowing, often most noticeable during inspiration and over the neck.

Why it matters

Stridor with respiratory distress, drooling, swelling, trauma, altered mental status, or rapidly worsening symptoms is an airway emergency warning sign.
