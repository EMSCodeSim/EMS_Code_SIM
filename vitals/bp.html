Learn How to Take a Manual Blood Pressure

Manual auscultated blood pressure

  1. Position the patient with the arm supported near heart level.
  2. Select the correct cuff and wrap it snugly over bare skin.
  3. Locate the brachial artery and place the stethoscope bell or diaphragm over it.
  4. Inflate above the expected systolic pressure.
  5. Deflate slowly and listen: the first repeated sound is systolic; disappearance of sounds is diastolic.
  6. Record systolic/diastolic in mmHg, the arm, position, and method when relevant.

Cuff sizing and positioning

Bladder width

About 40% of the arm circumference.

Bladder length

About 80% of the arm circumference.

Too small

Can produce a falsely high reading.

Too large

Can produce a reading that is lower than the true pressure.

Technique check: The artery marker should align with the brachial artery, the lower cuff edge should sit above the antecubital space, and clothing should not be trapped beneath the cuff.

How to obtain a palpated systolic pressure

1
Palpate the radial pulse while inflating the cuff.
2
Note the pressure where the radial pulse disappears.
3
Deflate completely and wait briefly before reinflating.
4
For an auscultated reading, inflate approximately 20–30 mmHg above the palpated systolic value, then deflate slowly.

A palpated pressure gives only the systolic value. Document it clearly, such as “BP 104/P” or according to local documentation practice.

Interpreting an adult reading

Normal: systolic below 120 and diastolic below 80
Elevated: systolic 120–129 and diastolic below 80
Stage 1 range: systolic 130–139 or diastolic 80–89
Stage 2 range: systolic at least 140 or diastolic at least 90

EMS interpretation depends on the entire patient: symptoms, trends, perfusion, medications, age, position, recent exertion, and the accuracy of the measurement. One number alone does not make a diagnosis.
