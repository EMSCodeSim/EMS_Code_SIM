Lesson 1

HOW: Obtain a SAMPLE history

Ask one clear question at a time, listen to the answer, and follow up on anything that changes care.

S

Signs and symptoms

Ask what the patient feels and identify what you can observe. Clarify the chief complaint before moving on.

A

Allergies

Ask about medication, food, environmental, and latex allergies. Document the reaction whenever possible.

M

Medications

Ask about prescriptions, over-the-counter drugs, inhalers, insulin, anticoagulants, and recently missed or changed doses.

P

Pertinent history

Ask about medical problems, surgeries, pregnancy, prior similar episodes, and baseline function relevant to today’s complaint.

L

Last oral intake

Ask what and when the patient last ate or drank. Include alcohol, medications, and other substances when relevant.

E

Events leading up

Ask what happened before symptoms began, what the patient was doing, and how the condition has changed.

Do not treat SAMPLE like a script

Change the order when needed. Life threats, pain, respiratory distress, altered mental status, and scene conditions may require immediate intervention before a complete history.

Useful follow-up questions

  • What reaction do you have to that allergy?
  • When was the last dose taken?
  • Is this normal for the patient?
  • Has this happened before?
  • What changed immediately before symptoms?

Common mistakes

  • Accepting “none” without clarification
  • Listing medications without their purpose
  • Ignoring anticoagulants or insulin
  • Failing to ask a caregiver or witness
  • Documenting conclusions instead of answers

Lesson 2

WHY: Understand why the history matters

SAMPLE helps explain the current findings, reveals risks, and identifies information that may change treatment or transport.

Allergies need reactions

“Allergic to penicillin” is incomplete. Rash, nausea, swelling, and anaphylaxis have very different implications.

Medications reveal disease

Medication lists may identify hidden conditions, treatment risks, overdose possibilities, and adherence problems.

History changes risk

Anticoagulants after a fall, pregnancy with abdominal pain, or insulin with altered mental status can immediately raise concern.

Events create context

Exertion, trauma, heat exposure, missed meals, new medication, or substance use can connect symptoms to a likely cause.
