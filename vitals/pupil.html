    How to use & clinical notes

    Eyes are labeled from the patient’s perspective: the eye on your left is the patient’s right, and vice versa. Toggle light to assess reactivity; choose conditions for each eye, then “Show Results.” The purple scale provides a visual mm reference for pupil size (approx. 6 px ≈ 1 mm in this sim).

    Common patterns

    • Pinpoint: opioids, pontine lesion, cholinergic agents.
    • Dilated reactive: sympathomimetics, anticholinergics, dark environment.
    • Fixed dilated: CN III compression (uncal herniation), mydriatic drops, severe anoxia.
    • Sluggish: rising ICP, hypoxia, hypothermia, sedatives.
    • Fixed mid: severe midbrain injury, deep anesthesia.

    Anisocoria tips

    • Compare in light and dark. Worse in light → larger pupil is abnormal (parasympathetic issue). Worse in dark → smaller pupil is abnormal (sympathetic issue).
    • Consider trauma, eye drops, headache/neck pain (carotid dissection), and neuro deficits.
