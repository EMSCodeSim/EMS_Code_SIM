Assessment sequence

Work the patient in the same order every time

The exact order can flex when an immediate life threat demands action, but this structure keeps important steps from being missed.

1

Scene Size-Up

Make sure the scene is safe before focusing on the patient.

  • PPE / standard precautions
  • Scene safety
  • Number of patients
  • Nature of illness or mechanism of injury
  • Additional resources
  • Spinal-motion concerns when indicated
First-look drill
2

Primary Assessment

Form a general impression, assess responsiveness, and identify immediate life threats.

  • General impression
  • AVPU / mental status
  • Airway
  • Breathing
  • Circulation / major bleeding
  • Patient priority and transport urgency
AirwayBreathingCirculation
3

History of Present Illness

Gather the information that changes risk, treatment, and transport decisions.

  • Chief complaint
  • SAMPLE history
  • OPQRST when a symptom needs focused clarification
  • Pertinent positives and negatives
SAMPLE simulatorOPQRST simulator
4

Secondary Assessment

Perform the focused exam that matches the complaint or mechanism.

  • Focused medical assessment
  • Rapid or focused trauma exam
  • Neurologic, chest, abdominal, or other complaint-specific exam
ChestAbdomenTrauma
5

Baseline Vital Signs

Measure, interpret, and document findings that can be trended during care.

  • Blood pressure
  • Pulse
  • Respirations
  • SpO₂
  • Skin signs
  • Glucose, temperature, pupils, or GCS when indicated
BPPulseRespirationsSpO₂
6

Treatment / Intervention

Treat findings you actually discovered and document what was done.

  • Airway and breathing support
  • Bleeding / shock care
  • Protocol-authorized medications
  • Positioning, splinting, or other EMT care
Treatment practice
7

Reassessment

Prove whether the patient is improving, worsening, or unchanged.

  • Repeat ABCs
  • Repeat key vital signs
  • Recheck the chief complaint
  • Assess response to treatment
  • Adjust transport or care priorities
Reassessment practice
8

Transport, PCR & Handoff

Package the important story so the receiving team understands what happened and what changed.

  • Working impression
  • Priority and destination
  • Pertinent history and exam findings
  • Vital-sign trends
  • Treatments and patient response
