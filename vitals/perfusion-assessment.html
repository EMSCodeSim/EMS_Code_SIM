Lesson 1

HOW: Perform a circulation and perfusion assessment

Control life-threatening bleeding immediately, then combine multiple findings rather than relying on one number.

1

Look for major bleeding

Scan the patient and surroundings for external blood loss. Control severe bleeding before completing the rest of the assessment.

2

Check a pulse

Assess rate, rhythm, strength, and location. Compare a peripheral pulse with a central pulse when perfusion is questionable.

3

Assess skin signs

Document color, temperature, and moisture. Pale, cool, clammy, mottled, or cyanotic skin may signal poor perfusion or another serious problem.

4

Check capillary refill

Use capillary refill as one supporting finding, especially in children. Consider temperature, age, lighting, and peripheral vascular disease.

5

Evaluate mental status and BP

Altered mentation and falling blood pressure can indicate worsening perfusion. A normal blood pressure does not rule out compensated shock.

6

Treat and trend

Control bleeding, maintain airway and breathing, prevent heat loss, position appropriately, expedite transport, and repeat your findings.

Think in patterns. Tachycardia, weak peripheral pulses, cool clammy skin, delayed refill, anxiety, and narrowing pulse pressure may appear before hypotension.

Lesson 2

WHY: Understand perfusion and shock

Perfusion is the delivery of oxygenated blood to tissues. Shock is inadequate tissue perfusion, regardless of the first blood-pressure reading.

Compensated shock

The body maintains blood pressure through tachycardia and vasoconstriction. Anxiety, cool skin, weak pulses, and delayed refill may appear early.

Decompensated shock

Compensation fails and blood pressure falls. Mental status, pulse quality, breathing, and skin findings often worsen rapidly.

Pulse pressure

A narrowing gap between systolic and diastolic pressure can support concern for worsening perfusion when combined with the patient presentation.

Central vs. peripheral pulse

A central pulse may remain palpable after a weak peripheral pulse disappears. This suggests reduced peripheral circulation and requires urgent reassessment.

Children compensate differently

Pediatric patients may maintain blood pressure until late. Appearance, heart rate, skin circulation, and work of breathing deserve close attention.

Trend beats snapshot

Repeated findings show whether perfusion is improving or deteriorating. Record times, interventions, and patient responses.
