EMT

Organize → Document → Report

Turn assessment findings into a clear patient story.

Review a complete EMT patient encounter, identify normal and abnormal findings, write an objective PCR narrative, and deliver a concise verbal handoff without adding facts that were never assessed.

0 of 3 lessons complete

Lesson 1

HOW: Build the patient-care story

1

Start with dispatch and presentation

State why EMS was called, where the patient was found, and the patient’s general appearance.

2

Document the assessment

Include relevant history, primary assessment, focused findings, and vital signs in a logical order.

3

Separate fact from impression

Write objective findings first. Label the diagnosis as a working or suspected clinical impression.

4

Record treatment precisely

Document what was done, the route or device when relevant, and the patient response.

5

Show reassessment

Compare repeat findings with the initial assessment and describe improvement, deterioration, or no change.

6

Give a focused handoff

Use MIST or IMIST-AMBO: patient, problem, key history, findings, treatment, response, and remaining concerns.

Document what you know—not what you assume. “Possible stroke” is appropriate. “Patient had a stroke” overstates what an EMT assessment can confirm.

Lesson 2

WHY: Good communication protects the patient

Continuity of care

The receiving team uses your timeline, findings, and treatment response to continue care safely.

Clinical reasoning

A structured narrative shows how findings supported your working impression and priorities.

Objective language

Specific observations are more useful than labels such as “looks bad,” “normal,” or “stable.”

Negative findings matter

Pertinent negatives can help distinguish between competing causes, but only document what was actually assessed.

Handoffs must be brief

Lead with the immediate problem and high-risk findings. Extra detail can follow after the urgent facts are understood.

Never invent

Missing information should be identified as unknown, not completed with a guess.
