Understand Capnography

How it works

  • Capnography measures CO₂ in exhaled air over time; ETCO₂ is the peak at end-exhalation.
  • Reflects ventilation, perfusion, and metabolism.
  • Use it to confirm airway device placement, titrate BVM rate, monitor CPR quality, and recognize ROSC.

Normal readings

  • Awake adult: 35–45 mmHg with a square waveform.
  • CPR: often 10–20 mmHg; a sustained jump toward the 30s suggests ROSC.
  • Adult BVM target: 10–12/min. Avoid hyperventilation.

High vs Low ETCO₂

  • High: hypoventilation, increased CO₂ production (fever/seizures), ROSC/improved perfusion, rebreathing/equipment fault.
  • Low: hyperventilation, poor perfusion (shock/PE/arrest), low-quality CPR, prolonged downtime, or esophageal/failed airway (near-zero, flatline).

EMT-B decision cues

  • Inadequate breathing: BVM + O₂ 10–12/min; titrate by ETCO₂.
  • Bronchospasm: Assist prescribed bronchodilator; BVM only if ventilation fails.
  • Airway device: Near-zero ETCO₂ → remove suspect device, OPA/NPA, BVM.
  • CPR: Use ETCO₂ to gauge compression quality & recognize ROSC; avoid hyperventilation.
  • Rebreathing: Elevated baseline → fix/replace equipment/valve.
