Communication and Professionalism

Practice patient introductions, team communication, radio reports, handoffs, objective language, and respectful behavior.

45–60 minutesSaved progressPre-course readiness

Foundation only

This module prepares you to begin approved EMT education. It does not provide certification, course credit, local protocols, medical direction, or authorization to perform patient care.

Core lesson

1. Patient-centered introductions

Introduce yourself and your role, explain what you are doing, ask permission when appropriate, preserve dignity, and use understandable language. Listen without interrupting, acknowledge concerns, and avoid promising outcomes. Use qualified interpreter resources when required rather than relying on children or bystanders for complex medical communication.

Core lesson

2. Team communication

Closed-loop communication means an instruction is directed, repeated back, completed, and confirmed. Speak up about hazards, changes, missing equipment, or uncertainty. Respectful clarification is safer than silent confusion. A good teammate communicates workload and helps without taking over every task.

Core lesson

3. Radio and hospital reports

A basic report identifies the unit, patient age category, chief concern, important history, assessment findings, vital trends, treatment, response, and estimated arrival. The order may vary locally. Practice delivering the important information in under a minute, then answer questions.

Core lesson

4. Objective documentation

Separate patient statements, witness statements, and your observations. Replace judgmental labels with observable behavior. “Uncooperative” may become “patient repeatedly pulled away and declined assessment after risks were explained.” Avoid unsupported conclusions and document changes over time.

Core lesson

5. Professionalism in difficult moments

Privacy, respectful language, appropriate social media use, honesty, punctuality, preparation, and receiving feedback all matter. Professionalism does not mean never feeling stress; it means managing behavior, asking for help, and protecting the patient even when a scene is frustrating.

Applied activity · about 8–10 minutes

Put the lesson into practice

Record yourself giving a 45-second fictional handoff. Listen once for missing facts and once for vague or judgmental wording.

Closed-loop communication reduces ambiguity by confirming that a message was heard and completed.

Objective documentation describes observed behavior and relevant statements rather than judgmental labels.
