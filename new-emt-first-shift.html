New EMT First Shift: What to Bring and What to Do

Arrive early, learn the ambulance, communicate before acting, and make safety and coachability your priorities—not trying to look experienced.

The dependable answer

Arrive early, learn the ambulance, communicate before acting, and make safety and coachability your priorities—not trying to look experienced.

Reddit discussions helped identify the repeated questions. The advice here is organized and reviewed by EMSCodeSim, not copied from individual posts.

What should I do before the shift?

Confirm reporting time, station, uniform, required credentials, parking, and who you should meet. Sleep as well as the schedule allows and avoid arriving on an empty tank. Pack water, meals, snacks, a phone charger, spare pens, a small notebook, and any required PPE or equipment.

  • Arrive 15–20 minutes early.
  • Introduce yourself to the crew and ask what they expect from you.
  • Do not carry a large personal gear collection unless your employer requires it.

What should I learn about the ambulance?

Learn where airway equipment, oxygen, hemorrhage-control supplies, splints, medications, monitor accessories, linens, pediatric gear, and PPE are kept. Ask how the cot, stair chair, power load, radio, fuel card, and unit check are handled.

Your first goal is not memorizing every drawer instantly. It is knowing how to find critical items and who to ask.

What should I do on calls?

Clarify your role before the first call. During care, complete assigned tasks, verbalize important findings, repeat abnormal vital signs, and tell your partner when you are uncertain. Never perform a skill without authorization or communication.

Watch how experienced clinicians introduce themselves, position the patient, gather history, manage family members, and prepare for movement.

How should I ask for feedback?

After an appropriate call, ask one focused question: “What should I repeat, and what should I change on the next call?” Write down patterns rather than every comment. Pick one correction to deliberately practice on the next opportunity.

What should a new EMT avoid?

  • Pretending to understand when you do not.
  • Standing back without asking how to help.
  • Touching equipment or patients without communication.
  • Arguing defensively about feedback.
  • Entering identifying patient details in personal notes.
  • Driving, lifting, or operating equipment beyond training.
