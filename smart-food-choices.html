For working EMTs & paramedics

Eat better when the rig becomes your break room.

You may not know when the next meal is coming. This guide helps crews make a better choice at common restaurants, pack food that survives a long shift, and keep useful snacks within reach when calls stack up.

Protein firstHelps meals stay satisfying
Add plantsFruit, vegetables, beans, whole grains
Watch liquid caloriesWater or unsweetened drinks most often
Plan the backupCarry food for the call you didn't expect
1. Pick the proteinGrilled chicken, eggs, lean meat, beans, or a protein bowl.
2. Add volumeVegetables, fruit, beans, or a side salad beat doubling the fries.
3. Control extrasSauces, cheese, creamy dressing, and sugary drinks can change the whole meal.
4. Don't chase perfectionA better choice you will actually eat is more useful than skipping food all shift.

Better picks at common stops

Menu availability changes. Use these as practical starting points and check the restaurant's current nutrition information when needed.

Chick-fil-A

Grilled Nuggets + fruit cup

Lean proteinEasy in the rig

The grilled nuggets are one of the easiest ways to get a protein-centered meal without a fried entrée. Add fruit or another produce side instead of fries.

Order it: grilled nuggets + fruit cup + water/unsweetened tea. Use dipping sauce lightly.

Official Chick-fil-A nutrition lists grilled nuggets at 130 calories for a 95 g serving; portions vary by count.

Chick-fil-A

Market Salad with Grilled Nuggets

32 g proteinProduce-heavy

A practical full meal when you want more produce and enough protein to make the salad filling.

Order it: Market Salad with grilled nuggets. If you are watching calories or sodium, use part of the dressing/toppings.

Current official listing: about 340 calories and 32 g protein, with standard included components.

Chipotle

Chicken bowl or salad

CustomizableBeans + vegetables

Build around chicken, black or pinto beans, fajita vegetables, tomato salsa and lettuce. Rice can fit—choose the amount based on how hungry and active you are.

Order it: chicken + beans + fajita vegetables + salsa + lettuce. Pick one richer extra such as cheese or guacamole instead of automatically stacking every add-on.

Chipotle provides a live nutrition calculator because totals depend heavily on your build.

Taco Bell

Cantina Chicken Bowl

25 g proteinBeans + vegetables

A bowl gives you chicken, black beans and vegetables without needing to make the meal mostly fried sides.

Order it: Cantina Chicken Bowl. To lighten it, reduce creamy sauce/sour cream or choose fewer extras.

Current Taco Bell listing: 575 calories and 25 g protein.

Wendy's

Grilled Chicken Ranch Wrap

28 g proteinOne-hand friendly

A practical high-protein choice when you may be eating between posts. Pair with apple bites or another lighter side rather than automatically adding fries.

Order it: grilled chicken ranch wrap + apple bites + water.

Wendy's 2026 high-protein guide lists the wrap at 28 g protein. Check current menu nutrition for full totals and local availability.

Wendy's

Cobb or Apple Pecan Salad

30–37 g proteinDressings add up

Both can be solid entrée choices. Use enough dressing to enjoy it without assuming the whole packet has to go on.

Order it: entrée salad + water/unsweetened drink. If the shift is very active, add a simple carb side rather than dessert.
McDonald's

Egg McMuffin

17 g protein310 calories

A comparatively compact breakfast with egg and Canadian bacon that can work well when the alternative is a large breakfast combo.

Order it: Egg McMuffin + black coffee/water. Add fruit you brought from the station or rig.
McDonald's

Hamburger + simple side

250 caloriesModest protein

If burgers are the only realistic option, a basic hamburger is much easier to fit into a balanced shift than a large double-burger combo.

Order it: hamburger + apple slices when available + unsweetened drink. If you need more food, add a second protein item thoughtfully instead of upsizing fries and soda.
Panera

Strawberry Poppyseed Chicken Salad

27 g protein8 g fiberWatch sodium

A produce-heavy option with chicken, especially useful when most of the shift has been convenience food.

Order it: whole salad if this is your full meal, or half with another lean item. Dressing and toppings can be adjusted.

Panera's June 2026 guide lists the whole salad at 410 calories, 27 g protein and 8 g fiber; sodium remains substantial at many restaurant meals.

Panera

Green Goddess Cobb with Chicken

40 g proteinHigh sodium

High protein and filling, but the standard whole salad is also high in sodium. It can still be useful if you know that tradeoff.

Order it: consider a half portion or lighter use of dressing/toppings if sodium or total intake matters to you.
Starbucks

Eggs & Cheddar Protein Box

22 g proteinFruit included

A genuinely useful no-planning emergency meal: eggs, cheese, fruit and a grain component in one portable box.

Order it: protein box + unsweetened coffee/tea or water.
Starbucks

Turkey Bacon, Cheddar & Egg White Sandwich

17 g proteinBreakfast

Another compact breakfast that avoids turning a coffee stop into a pastry-only meal.

Order it: sandwich + coffee with minimal added sugar. Add a piece of fruit if available.
Subway

6-inch Fresh Fit / lean-protein sub

20+ g proteinVegetable-friendly

Use a six-inch sandwich with turkey or chicken, pile on vegetables, and go lighter on cheese and creamy sauces.

Order it: lean protein + whole-grain bread when available + plenty of vegetables + mustard/vinegar-style sauce.

Subway's Fresh Fit program identifies participating six-inch sandwiches with 20 g or more protein, a serving of vegetables, and fewer than 500 calories.

Subway

Protein bowl

High proteinLower bread load

A useful choice if you want a vegetable-heavy meal and would rather get your carbohydrate from a side you brought or later in the shift.

Order it: rotisserie-style chicken or turkey bowl, extra vegetables, lighter sauce.

Rig meals that actually work

The goal is not meal-prep perfection. Pack food that can survive interrupted meals, delayed posts and an uncertain return to the station.

🧊

Cooler: chicken grain bowl

  • Chicken or beans
  • Brown rice/quinoa
  • Roasted vegetables
  • Salsa or light dressing

Good cold or reheated. Portion into a sealed container.

🌯

Cooler: turkey hummus wrap

  • Whole-grain tortilla
  • Turkey
  • Hummus
  • Spinach/peppers

Easy to eat without balancing a bowl on the steering wheel.

🥣

Thermos: chili

  • Lean turkey/beef or beans
  • Tomatoes
  • Beans
  • Vegetables

High protein + fiber and forgiving if meal time keeps moving.

🥚

Cooler: EMS bento box

  • Hard-boiled eggs
  • Cheese
  • Fruit
  • Whole-grain crackers
  • Raw vegetables

Works well when you only get five minutes at a time.

🥛

Cooler: overnight oats

  • Oats
  • Greek yogurt or milk
  • Berries
  • Chia/flax
  • Nuts

Breakfast or a late-shift meal that does not depend on a microwave.

🥫

No cooler: tuna/chicken kit

  • Shelf-stable tuna/chicken pouch
  • Whole-grain crackers
  • Applesauce/whole fruit
  • Nuts

Keep unopened shelf-stable items as your “calls never stopped” backup.

🥜

No cooler: PB wrap

  • Whole-grain tortilla
  • Peanut/almond butter
  • Banana when practical
  • Cinnamon

Cheap, fast and easy. Use shelf-stable nut butter packets for your backup bag.

🌱

No cooler: protein snack meal

  • Roasted chickpeas/edamame
  • Nuts
  • Whole fruit
  • Whole-grain crackers

Useful for crews without reliable refrigeration, especially as an emergency reserve.

🍲

Thermos: soup + protein

  • Bean/lentil soup
  • Chicken vegetable soup
  • Whole-grain crackers
  • Fruit on the side

Preheat the thermos and follow safe hot-holding practices.

🚑 Ambulance food-safety rule

Do not treat the cab like a refrigerator. USDA guidance says cold perishable food should stay at 40°F or below. Food should generally not remain unrefrigerated for more than 2 hours, or 1 hour when temperatures are above 90°F. Use an insulated lunch bag/cooler with adequate frozen gel packs. Follow your agency's infection-control rules and keep food separated from patient-care equipment and contaminated areas.

Pack the shift in layers

Meal 1
A real protein-centered meal.
Meal 2 / backup
Something cold, thermos-safe or shelf stable.
2 quick snacks
One protein + one fruit/whole-grain option.
Water
Bring a refillable bottle and actually keep it accessible.

Snacks for stacked calls

Choose snacks that solve hunger rather than just giving you something sweet to do at post.

🥜

Nuts + fruit

Portable, no prep, and combines fat/protein with carbohydrate. Use single portions if the full bag disappears without you noticing.

🥛

Greek yogurt

High-protein option when you have a properly chilled lunch bag. Add berries or a small amount of granola.

🍎

Apple + nut butter

Cheap, durable, and easy to keep as a backup. Single-serve nut butter packets reduce mess.

🌱

Roasted edamame/chickpeas

Shelf-stable crunch with more protein and fiber than many chips.

🧀

Cheese + whole-grain crackers

Works well in a cooler. Add fruit or vegetables for a more complete mini-meal.

🥩

Jerky + fruit

Portable protein, but many jerkies are very high in sodium. Compare labels and pair with water and fruit.

🍿

Plain/light popcorn

A higher-volume snack when you want something crunchy. Pair with protein if this needs to carry you for hours.

🥚

Hard-boiled eggs

Simple protein when kept safely cold. Pack peeled eggs in a sealed container.

🍫

Protein bar—read the label

Useful emergency backup, not automatically “healthy.” Compare protein, fiber, added sugar and total calories rather than the front-of-package claims.

Late-call rule

If you know you are ravenous after midnight, plan for it before the shift. A prepared sandwich, yogurt, fruit, nuts or protein box makes it easier to avoid turning every late hospital drop into a large drive-through meal.

How this guide is built

Restaurant menus and nutrition change. Menu examples were checked against current official restaurant information on August 9, 2026, including Chick-fil-A, Chipotle, Taco Bell, Wendy's, McDonald's, Panera, Starbucks and Subway. Food-safety guidance follows USDA/FDA cold-holding and two-hour rules.

Educational use: “healthier” is relative and individual nutrition needs differ. This page is not individualized medical or dietary advice. People with diabetes, kidney/heart disease, food allergies, pregnancy-related needs, or other medical concerns should follow their clinician's guidance.
