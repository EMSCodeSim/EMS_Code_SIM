The core method

Inspect, touch, compare, and document

1

Prepare the view

Use adequate lighting, expose the relevant area, and reduce color distortion from shadows or colored lights.

2

Establish baseline

Ask what is normal, compare multiple sites, and look for a change rather than expecting one universal normal shade.

3

Inspect color

Check useful locations such as lips, oral mucosa, conjunctiva, palms, nail beds, face, and exposed extremities.

4

Feel temperature

Use the back of your hand, compare central with peripheral areas, and compare right with left when needed.

5

Assess moisture

Describe dry, normal, moist, or diaphoretic and decide whether it fits the environment and patient activity.

6

Check perfusion

Use capillary refill when appropriate, then interpret it with pulses, mental status, BP, temperature, and the rest of the exam.

What part of the assessment are you doing?

Color: compare with baseline

Inspect several areas rather than relying on one location. Use lips, oral mucosa, conjunctiva, palms, nail beds, and exposed skin, then compare with the patient’s usual appearance.

Capillary-refill activity

Press, release, and watch color return

This animation teaches the concept. Real capillary refill is affected by site, pressure, lighting, temperature, age, and technique.

Ready

How to check

  1. Choose an appropriate site and note temperature.
  2. Apply enough pressure to blanch the tissue.
  3. Release and measure the return of color.
  4. Document the observed time and site.
  5. Interpret it with the full perfusion assessment.

Press the button to start the animation.

Important: A delayed result is a clue, not a stand-alone diagnosis. Cold skin and inconsistent technique can prolong refill.
