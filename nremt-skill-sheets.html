Patient assessmentMedical

Patient Assessment/Management—Medical

Practice moving from scene size-up through primary assessment, focused history/exam, vitals, intervention, reassessment, and report.

  1. PPE, scene safety, NOI, patient count, resources, and spinal need
  2. General impression, responsiveness, ABCs, and patient priority
  3. History, secondary assessment, baseline vitals, care, reassessment, and report

Patient assessmentTrauma

Patient Assessment/Management—Trauma

Use a rapid, threat-focused sequence while balancing spinal precautions, transport priority, vital signs, SAMPLE history, and reassessment.

  1. Control hazards and identify mechanism, patient count, resources, and spinal concerns
  2. Find and treat immediate airway, breathing, hemorrhage, and shock threats
  3. Complete rapid trauma assessment, vitals, history, transport, and reassessment

Airway and oxygenBVM

Bag-Valve-Mask Ventilation—Apneic Adult

Review body-substance isolation, responsiveness, airway opening, adjunct use, oxygen connection, mask seal, ventilation rate, and chest rise.

  1. Open and clear the airway
  2. Select and insert the appropriate adjunct
  3. Ventilate with oxygen while confirming visible chest rise

Airway and oxygenOxygen

Oxygen Administration by Non-Rebreather Mask

Practice cylinder safety, regulator setup, flow selection, reservoir inflation, mask placement, and reassessment.

  1. Inspect and safely operate the oxygen system
  2. Set an appropriate flow and prefill the reservoir
  3. Apply the mask and reassess oxygenation and tolerance

CardiacAED

Cardiac Arrest Management/AED

Rehearse immediate CPR, AED preparation, safe rhythm analysis, shock delivery when advised, and rapid return to compressions.

  1. Recognize arrest and begin high-quality CPR
  2. Apply the AED while minimizing interruptions
  3. Clear for analysis/shock and resume CPR immediately

Trauma skillsSpinal

Spinal Immobilization—Seated Patient

Review manual stabilization, neurologic checks, device placement, torso/head securing, movement, and post-procedure reassessment.

  1. Maintain neutral manual stabilization
  2. Assess motor, sensory, and distal circulation
  3. Secure the patient and repeat the neurologic assessment

Trauma skillsSpinal

Spinal Immobilization—Supine Patient

Practice coordinated movement, neutral stabilization, securing torso and extremities, head immobilization, and reassessment.

  1. Direct the team and maintain stabilization
  2. Move and secure the patient in a controlled sequence
  3. Repeat motor, sensory, and circulatory findings

Trauma skillsBleeding/shock

Bleeding Control/Shock Management

Prioritize major hemorrhage control, shock recognition, oxygen/ventilatory support when indicated, heat conservation, and rapid transport.

  1. Expose and control life-threatening bleeding
  2. Escalate pressure, packing, or tourniquet use as appropriate
  3. Treat for shock, prevent heat loss, and reassess

Trauma skillsSplinting

Joint Injury Immobilization

Assess the injury and distal neurovascular status, stabilize in the position found when appropriate, secure the splint, and reassess.

  1. Check distal pulse, motor function, and sensation
  2. Immobilize the injured joint and adjacent structures
  3. Repeat distal findings after splinting

Trauma skillsSplinting

Long Bone Injury Immobilization

Expose and assess the extremity, check distal function, stabilize the bone and adjacent joints, then repeat the distal assessment.

  1. Manually stabilize and assess distal neurovascular function
  2. Apply the correct splint without creating additional injury
  3. Secure and reassess pulse, motor function, and sensation
