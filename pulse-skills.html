Picture guide

Common pulse locations

Different pulse sites answer different assessment questions. Start with the radial pulse for routine assessment and know the central and pediatric sites as well.

Radial pulse: the everyday workhorse

Place your index and middle fingers on the thumb side of the inner wrist. Use light pressure and avoid using your thumb.

Use: A good site for routine pulse rate, rhythm, and quality in a conscious patient.

How to take it

A clean beginner pulse sequence

1

Rest and position

For a resting pulse, let the patient rest. Support the arm when using the radial site.

2

Use two fingers

Use the index and middle fingers—not the thumb, which has its own pulse.

3

Press lightly

Too much pressure can compress the artery and make the pulse disappear.

4

Count the rate

Count 30 seconds and multiply by 2 when regular. Count a full 60 seconds when irregular.

5

Assess quality

Describe rhythm, strength, and equality—not only the rate.

6

Document

Record BPM, regular/irregular, strength, site, equality if compared, and relevant patient context.

Tap-to-measure

Tap the rhythm you feel

Tap once for each pulse beat. The tool estimates the rate and whether your tapping intervals appear regular.

Tap with each beat you feel. After several taps, the page estimates the rate and regularity.

This is useful for learning the relationship between beat spacing and BPM. It is not a medical rhythm detector.

When a real pulse is irregular, count for a full minute and document the irregularity.
