Look, feel, compare, and connect the finding to the whole patient

Skin signs are fast clues about circulation, oxygenation, temperature regulation, hydration, and illness. They become useful when you compare with baseline and combine them with mental status, pulse, breathing, blood pressure, and the history.

Four parts of skin assessment

Do not stop at “warm and dry”

C

Color

Compare the patient with their baseline and inspect several useful locations. Describe the change rather than assuming one shade looks the same on everyone.

T

Temperature

Use the back of your hand, compare central with peripheral areas, and compare sides when circulation is a concern.

M

Moisture

Unexpected diaphoresis, very dry skin, or changes that do not fit the environment can add important context.

CRT

Capillary refill

Use it as one peripheral-perfusion clue. Cold, lighting, site, age, and technique can affect what you see.
