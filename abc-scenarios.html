Before the cases

Use the same thinking pattern every time

1) Identify the immediate threat. 2) Provide the appropriate intervention within training and protocol. 3) Reassess the airway, breathing, circulation, mental status, and response. 4) Continue the full assessment and make the transport/resource decision.

These scenarios use terms already introduced in the ABC assessment and skills pages. They are designed for guided application—not diagnosis or independent treatment authorization.

Scenario set

Choose the best immediate priority

Scenario 1: Gurgling trauma patient

An unresponsive trauma patient has gurgling respirations and blood visible in the mouth. A carotid pulse is present.

Scenario 2: Tiring respiratory patient

A patient with severe respiratory distress is now drowsy. Respirations are shallow, chest rise is poor, and wheezing is barely audible.

Scenario 3: Hidden shock

After a fall, a patient is restless, pale, cool, and clammy with a rapid weak pulse. The initial blood pressure is still 112/72.

Scenario 4: Sudden collapse

An adult collapses, is unresponsive, is only gasping, and has no pulse.

Scenario 5: Allergic reaction

A patient has facial swelling, hoarse speech, wheezing, hives, and a weak rapid pulse after eating.

Scenario 6: Pulse present, breathing absent

An unresponsive patient has a definite pulse but is not breathing normally.
