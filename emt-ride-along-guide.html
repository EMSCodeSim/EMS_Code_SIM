EMT Ride-Along Guide: How to Prepare and Learn

Bring less gear than you think, communicate with the crew, stay involved within your allowed role, and use each call to connect classroom concepts with real patient care.

The dependable answer

Bring less gear than you think, communicate with the crew, stay involved within your allowed role, and use each call to connect classroom concepts with real patient care.

Reddit discussions helped identify the repeated questions. The advice here is organized and reviewed by EMSCodeSim, not copied from individual posts.

What should I bring?

Bring your required uniform and identification, a small notebook, two pens, water, food, snacks, charger, and any school-required equipment. A stethoscope and shears may be useful if required, but the agency may have equipment available.

What should I ask at the beginning?

Introduce yourself and ask: “What would you like me to do today?” Clarify what you may perform, what requires direct supervision, where to sit, and how the crew prefers students to assist with checks and cleanup.

How do I participate without getting in the way?

Stand where you can observe without blocking movement. Volunteer for vital signs, equipment retrieval, carrying, cleanup, and patient interviews only when invited. Communicate before touching the patient or equipment.

What questions should I ask?

  • What finding changed your level of concern?
  • Why did you choose that assessment order?
  • What would make you request additional resources?
  • How did you decide the safest way to move the patient?
  • What should I review after this call?

Save detailed questions until patient care and documentation are complete.

What are common ride-along mistakes?

Avoid disappearing into your phone, sleeping without permission, telling stories during patient care, asking crews about their “worst call,” oversharing patient details, or treating support tasks as beneath you. Confidentiality continues after the shift.
