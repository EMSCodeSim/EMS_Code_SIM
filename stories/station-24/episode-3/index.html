Chapter 1

The Regular

07:18 — Station 24

On his second shift, T.J. Brooks arrived with a smaller gear bag and a much larger coffee. Neither change went unnoticed.

“Look at that,” Ben Walker said. “He's evolving.”

“I brought only what I need.”

Mac Sullivan glanced at the bag. “You brought three flashlights.”

“One's a backup.”

“And the third?”

T.J. looked at his coffee. “For emotional support.”

The tones dropped before Ben could respond. Medic 24, sick person, apartment complex on the east side. Dispatch added that the caller was a seventy-two-year-old woman reporting weakness.

Mac recognized the address. “Mrs. Alvarez. We've been there a few times.”

“What's wrong with her?”

“Different things. Sometimes she needs help getting up. Sometimes she's worried about her breathing. We'll find out.”

At the ambulance, T.J. heard someone from the engine crew say, “Our frequent flyer.”

He laughed a little, mostly because everyone else seemed to understand the joke. Then he climbed into the passenger seat.

Mac didn't laugh. He handed T.J. the tablet.

“Read today's notes. Not yesterday's.”

Chapter 2

The Blue Chair

07:31 — Apartment 206

Mrs. Alvarez opened the door wearing slippers and a cardigan. A small dog immediately began conducting an inspection of T.J.'s boots.

“I'm sorry,” she said. “I know you boys are busy.”

“You called because you needed something,” Mac replied. “Tell us about it.”

The apartment was immaculate except for a pile of unopened mail and a cup of tea beside a blue armchair. Mrs. Alvarez sat down slowly. T.J. noticed she had to stop talking to catch her breath.

“Just tired,” she said. “I didn't sleep much.”

“Any trouble breathing?”

“Only when I get up.”

T.J. asked about chest discomfort, recent illness, medications and what had changed. She denied chest pain. She had been coughing for several days and had barely eaten since yesterday.

Mac asked whether she normally needed help walking to the bathroom.

“No. I usually take the dog out myself.”

The dog, apparently dissatisfied with the crew's progress, sat on Mac's boot.

T.J. put the pulse oximeter on her finger and waited for a reliable signal. Her oxygen saturation was 89 percent on room air. Her respiratory rate was 26. Her pulse was fast, and her blood pressure was lower than he expected.

He repeated the blood pressure manually.

“Mac, ninety-four over sixty.”

Mac moved closer. “Okay. Let's get her on oxygen and keep assessing.”

Mrs. Alvarez looked at the equipment. “I don't want to make a fuss.”

“You're not,” T.J. said. This time he meant it.

Chapter 3

Something Different

07:43 — On Scene

Mac listened to her lungs and obtained a cardiac tracing while T.J. helped with oxygen and gathered her medication list. They checked her temperature and blood glucose. The findings suggested an acute illness rather than simply a bad night's sleep.

“Could be an infection,” Mac said quietly. “Could be something else. We need the hospital to sort it out.”

T.J. looked at the blue chair. He wondered how many times a crew had stood in exactly this spot.

Mrs. Alvarez asked if they could just leave her with the oxygen.

Mac explained what concerned him, why he recommended transport, and what could happen if her condition worsened. He asked her to explain back what she understood. She was alert, able to discuss the risks, and clearly worried about something other than the hospital.

“My dog,” she finally said. “My daughter lives forty minutes away. Nobody has a key.”

T.J. crouched beside the little animal. Its collar said PICKLES.

“Pickles is the problem?”

“He needs his medicine at noon.”

Mac looked at T.J. “Let's see what we can arrange.”

With Mrs. Alvarez's permission, T.J. called her daughter. A neighbor she trusted agreed to look after Pickles until the daughter arrived. The crew confirmed the arrangement before leaving.

Mrs. Alvarez finally nodded. “All right. I'll go.”

T.J. helped prepare the stretcher. He had spent weeks memorizing signs and symptoms. Nobody had put a dog named Pickles on the exam.

Yet without solving that problem, they might not have gotten their patient to the hospital.

Chapter 4

A Different Kind of Quiet

08:02 — En Route

Mrs. Alvarez's breathing improved somewhat with oxygen, but she remained weak. Mac continued reassessment and treatment according to protocol, and T.J. recorded the findings and helped prepare the receiving hospital report.

“Do you have children?” she asked T.J.

“No, ma'am.”

“You look about twelve.”

Mac coughed into his sleeve.

“I'm twenty-two,” T.J. said.

“Oh. Well, that's better.”

She closed her eyes briefly, then opened them again when T.J. spoke to her. He kept checking her mental status and watching for changes.

At the emergency department, Mac reported the abnormal vital signs, the recent cough, the change from baseline, and the response to care. The receiving nurse moved quickly to continue evaluation.

Before the crew left, Mrs. Alvarez caught T.J.'s sleeve.

“Thank you for calling my daughter.”

He had expected her to thank him for the oxygen or the stretcher or something involving a medical device.

“Of course,” he said.

Outside the room, Mac asked him what had made the call different from what he'd expected.

“She was actually sick.”

Mac stopped walking.

T.J. heard his own words and wished he could pull them back.

“I mean — I thought because she'd called before…”

“People who call often can get sick too,” Mac said. “Sometimes knowing their baseline helps us catch it. Sometimes familiarity gets in our way.”

T.J. nodded.

“Don't let somebody else's shortcut become your assessment.”

Chapter 5

The Empty Coffee Cup

09:26 — Station 24

Back at the station, Elena Ramirez was standing at the counter with a mug that read WORLD'S OKAYEST PARAMEDIC.

“How's the rookie?”

“He's learning about dogs,” Mac said.

T.J. told her about Mrs. Alvarez, leaving out identifying details that weren't needed for the discussion. Elena listened, then said, “A patient can have a medical problem and a life problem at the same time. We don't always get to fix both, but we should notice both.”

Ben opened the refrigerator.

“Anybody seen my breakfast burrito?”

Mac pointed toward the microwave.

Ben opened it. Inside sat a plate with nothing but a folded napkin.

The room went silent.

T.J. raised his hand. “Before anyone makes assumptions, I'd like a complete assessment of the situation.”

Elena nearly spilled her coffee.

Ben stared at him. “You're getting too comfortable.”

Later, T.J. finished his report. He deleted the phrase “well-known patient” from his opening sentence and replaced it with the reason for today's call, her reported symptoms and the objective findings. He included the transport discussion and the arrangement made for her dog.

Mac reviewed the narrative.

“Better,” he said. “Tell the next clinician what they need to know, not what the station thinks it knows.”

T.J. saved the report.

The radio sounded again. Another sick person, another apartment, another name he didn't recognize.

He grabbed his bag.

“Think it's serious?” he asked.

Mac opened the ambulance door. “Let's go find out.”

This time, T.J. didn't need to be reminded.
